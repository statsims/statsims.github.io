Random Numbers and Distribution Explorer

Generate random draws and watch a sample build toward its theoretical distribution. The same numbers appear four ways simultaneously so you can connect random draws to histograms, cumulative curves, Q-Q plots, and mean convergence.

Sample vs. Population
Random draws are observations from a generating rule. The sample histogram converges toward the theoretical shape as n grows but never matches it exactly.
Four Representations
Histogram, ECDF, Q-Q plot, and running-mean chart display the same data. Connecting these views is the core skill this tool is designed to build.
Law of Large Numbers
The sample mean is noisy at small n but converges toward the theoretical expected value as draws accumulate. Convergence is gradual, not instant.
Shape Determines Story
Skew, modality, and discreteness change what the mean and median communicate. A single number can describe a distribution poorly or miss its structure entirely.

Key Interactions

  • Use +1 / +10 / +100 to add draws one at a time and watch the sample build.
  • Move the Probe slider in Explore to read cumulative proportions from the ECDF.
  • Toggle Show theoretical to compare your sample to the population rule.

What to Look For

  • At small n, all charts are noisy and irregular. That is expected, not an error.
  • On Convergence, watch the running mean narrow and settle toward the theoretical value.
  • For bimodal data, notice where the single mean lands relative to the two peaks.

Full Reference

Histogram

Frequency is the raw count of draws in each bin. Relative frequency is count divided by n, and converges to the theoretical density as n grows. For discrete distributions each bar represents one exact outcome; bin count does not apply.

ECDF (Empirical Cumulative Distribution Function)

The ECDF answers: what fraction of draws are at or below x? It rises in steps of 1/n, one step per observation. The probe drops a marker at a chosen x and reads the cumulative proportion from the y-axis.

ECDF(x) = (number of values ≤ x) / n

The upper-tail proportion is 1 minus the cumulative proportion at the same value.

Spread Parameter by Distribution

  • Normal: spread = standard deviation σ.
  • Uniform: spread = half-range; values span 50 ± 2·spread.
  • Right-skewed: spread = scale; larger values lengthen the right tail.
  • Binomial: slider sets success probability p, not variability directly.
  • Fair six-sided die: always fixed on {1,2,3,4,5,6}; no spread parameter.
  • Bimodal: spread = within-group SD; mode separation is a separate slider.

Q-Q Plot

A quantile-quantile plot places observed quantiles on the y-axis and theoretical quantiles on the x-axis. Points on the 45-degree reference line indicate the sample matches the theoretical distribution.

Common departures: an upward bow at the right end signals a longer right tail than theory predicts (right skew). An S-shape indicates heavier or lighter tails. Stepped patterns are normal for discrete distributions.

Plotting positions use the Hazen formula (i − 0.5)/n for order statistic i.

Draws and Running Mean

Each individual draw is plotted as a dot against its draw number. The running mean (red line) is the sample mean of all draws up to that point. The dashed blue line is the theoretical expected value.

By the Law of Large Numbers, the running mean converges to the theoretical mean as n increases. The convergence is not monotone; it oscillates and narrows.

Running mean(k) = (x₁ + ... + xₖ) / k → μ

Skewness

The bias-corrected third standardized moment. Positive = right tail; negative = left tail; near zero = approximate symmetry.

g₁ = [n/((n−1)(n−2))] ⋅ ∑((x−̅x)/s)³
